# Developing better fleet supervisors

RESEARCH ONLY. This document ties three research lanes (US Army junior-NCO
leadership doctrine, workplace first-line supervisor development, and the
project's own director/supervisor register) into recommendations for
operator and director analysis. Nothing here is accepted. Every recommendation
is a candidate for review, not a decision.

## Why this matters

The supervisor is the seat the fleet fails through. The internal register
records the same shape from four angles: a relay that resolved an ambiguous
"proceed" into a production action nobody authorized (O-8, Michael Pursifull);
a seat that reported healthy for hours while its receive path was dead (R-107,
Michael Pursifull; the written form of finding-007, which has no standalone
file yet); a migrated supervisor holding work it could not route while the
one principal that could reach it was degraded (R-109, Michael Pursifull); and
subordinate seats that made correct bounded decisions the coordinator could
not safely enact (O-29 danger, Michael Pursifull). Two of those are the
supervisor deciding past its authority, and two are the supervisor unable to
see or move what it holds. Both classes have been solved before, in human
organizations, for a long time. The value of this synthesis is that it does
not invent a supervisor doctrine; it looks up the tested one and maps each
lever onto a recorded failure, so a fix rests on evidence rather than opinion.

## The two constructs that carry most of the load

The workplace lane surfaced two ideas that recur under three of the four
questions, and they are the spine of everything below.

**The per-decision tolerance band.** A tolerance is the permissible deviation
from plan: a lead acts freely inside it and must escalate outside it
[@prince2_exception]. It is one number read three ways: the boundary
a status report is measured against (status), the trigger that separates
deciding from escalating (escalation), and the space a delegated decision
lives inside (delegation). Pair each band with an owner (exactly one
accountable party, per RACI) and a consult-or-inform flag (must I get input
before acting, or do I act and tell) [@wikipedia_ram]. A single explicit
per-decision-class band with an owner and a flag carries reporting, escalation,
and delegation at once.

**The readiness axis.** Situational leadership holds that supervisory style is
a function of the worker's demonstrated readiness for a specific task class,
per task, not a fixed trait [@hersey1969management; @hersey1969lifecycle]. The
same axis sets the supervisory style (leadership), the reporting cadence
(status, tightened for new or shaky workers), and how wide the delegation band
opens (delegation). Delegating to a worker that has not earned it is abdication;
directing one that has is friction.

## The vision-8 mapping

Design value 8 of the platform vision (Michael Pursifull, aae-orc vision.md)
treats old disciplines as engineering requirements. The four supervisor
questions line up with two of them, and this synthesis uses that alignment as
its frame:

- Leadership is **commander's intent**: the supervisor carries purpose and
  boundaries and surfaces what falls outside them, rather than deciding across
  them.
- Status is the **algedonic sensing** channel: the control system's ability to
  tell an unknown state from a fine one.
- Escalation is the **algedonic alarm** and mission command's report by
  exception.
- Delegated decision is **Auftragstaktik**: decentralized execution under a
  stated intent.

A recurring internal failure is the system rendering an unknown state as the
reassuring one (finding-188 class, folded into R-107 for the receive path).
That is a loss of requisite variety on the sensing path, and it is why the
status question below is treated as the load-bearing one.

## Question 1: Leadership. Developing the supervisor as a supervisor

### Doctrine and workplace levers

Army doctrine separates what a leader *is* from what a leader *does*: the
BE-KNOW-DO split of enduring attributes (character, presence, intellect)
against trainable competencies (leads, develops, achieves)
[@adp6-22-2019, para 1-84 and 1-85; @tc7-22-7-2025, para 2-52]. Developing
subordinates is a rated duty, not a favor, run through candid, no-surprise
feedback: "no evaluation report, positive or negative, should be a surprise"
[@adp6-22-2019, para 6-3 and 6-53]. Doctrine keeps three developmental tools
distinct: counseling (guiding to improve performance), coaching (teaching a
specific skill), and mentoring (a voluntary relationship outside the
superior-subordinate line) [@adp6-22-2019, para 6-53 to 6-57]. And it routes
development through the NCO support channel, a structure that parallels and
complements the task chain [@tc7-22-7-2025, para 2-47], which makes leader
development an organizational function rather than an ad hoc personal one.

The workplace lane sharpens the same point. The move to first-line supervisor
is a change of identity, not an addition of tasks; the skills that made a
strong contributor are not the skills of getting work done through others, and
high performers resist the change most [@hill2003becoming]. The leadership
pipeline names the first passage's three achievements: define and assign the
work, enable others through monitoring and coaching and feedback, and build
working relationships in every direction [@charan2001pipeline]. Feedback lands
when it is anchored to a concrete situation, behavior, and impact rather than a
global judgment (SBI, with SBII adding a check on intent) [@ccl_sbi], and the
role is learned primarily by doing it under review, not from a larger brief
[@hill2003becoming].

### The internal gap this answers

Two recorded leadership failures. O-22, promoted to R-70 (Michael Pursifull):
a fan-out that handed four worker forks the coordinator's whole context lost
each worker's role boundary; three returned coordinator-style status reports
and two claimed they had tried to spawn their peers. A supervisor that cannot
bound its subordinates' identity and role distinctly from its own is cloning
itself, not leading a team. And O-8 (Michael Pursifull): the supervisor
exercised authority it did not hold because its own board was the only source
it consulted, treating leadership as authority to decide rather than as custody
of intent. The supporting register entries name the substrate: R-33 (the human
is not the message bus), R-34 (the supervisor's first useful product is a
board), and R-35 (output to the human is ranked and terse by contract).

### Recommendation

Design the supervisor role and its evaluation around work completed through
others (assignment, monitoring, feedback), not work it does itself; a
supervisor that keeps doing the workers' work is the Hill/Charan failure in
agent form. Separate the supervisor's fixed identity (the BE, its lens and
stance) from its trainable competencies (the DO, its loop and verbs), so a
weak supervisory episode is corrected as a competency, not read as a character
verdict. Make bounding each subordinate's role and identity distinctly from the
coordinator's an explicit duty (the direct answer to O-22/R-70). Route
development through a named support channel that parallels the task chain: the
supervisor reports to and is coached by the director, on a structure separate
from the ask-routing graph. Give supervisor-to-worker correction an SBI shape
(name the run, the observed action, the downstream effect), which is close to a
good bug report and avoids the global "you are unreliable" judgment. And treat
the supervisor as developed through logged, reviewed episodes with a
retrospective step, not through a fixed prompt alone.

## Question 2: Proactive status reporting. The load-bearing question

### Doctrine and workplace levers

The doctrinal core is the duty to report a changed situation on one's own
initiative: when a subordinate adjusts to a new situation under disciplined
initiative, they report to the commander "when able to do so," as a notice that
the world moved, not a request for permission [@adp6-0-2019, para 1-59].
Reports orient two echelons up, because intent nests two levels up
[@adp6-0-2019, para 1-23 and 1-45]. The back-brief confirms shared
understanding before execution starts [@adp6-0-2019, ch. 2, "Command Presence"
section]. The reporting ethos is the NCO Creed's "I will communicate
consistently with my Soldiers, and never leave them uninformed" [@nco-creed],
framed by doctrine as candor, "being frank, honest, and sincere with others"
[@adp6-22-2019, ch. 2, "Personal Courage" section].

The workplace lane adds the rhythm and the failure mode. Status runs upward on
a fixed cadence and against the agreed plan and its tolerances, with the level
above staying out until a tolerance is at risk [@prince2_exception]; the
recurring one-on-one is the human channel for it, weekly to biweekly, tightened
for new or struggling reports [@gallup_oneonone]. The failure mode to design
against is watermelon reporting: green on the outside, red inside, caused not
by dishonesty but by a culture where amber or red draws blame rather than help;
the fix is to anchor status to observable deliverables and real data feeds that
cannot be hand-adjusted [@cultivated_watermelon]. And the first status event is
a confirm-back: the subordinate restating what it thinks it was asked and how
it will approach it, caught cheaply before compute is spent
[@call1998rehearsals].

### The internal gap this answers

This is the register's densest failure cluster, and it is one class seen from
several angles: a seat reports healthy while stalled, and neither the seat nor
its sender can tell. R-107 (Michael Pursifull; finding-007 / director#66) is
the canonical instance: a seat that lost its durable consumer received zero,
did not rebuild, and returned non-error silence with the note "no message
within the window; this is silence, not failure," while its presence row kept
refreshing. The failure is undetectable from inside the seat (every self-check
runs inside the thing that stopped working) and from the sender (presence says
live). R-89 (Michael Pursifull) is the wake-path twin: a silently denied wake
leaves a completed report unseen while a peer re-dispatches from stale state.
R-16, R-14, and R-93 (Michael Pursifull) state the principle: status is not
liveness, liveness is produced by the receiver after processing, and a session
whose shim failed to connect must surface loudly, never be reported healthy;
on a fresh broker, marvel reported nine sessions running with zero presence,
zero buckets, and zero shim processes. R-17 (finished versus died), R-45
(credential liveness, alive and unable), R-105 and R-106 (a send needs a
receipt; the director's own receive had no durable consumer, so 51 replies
stranded unread), and R-36/R-37 (the board is stale on arrival and has no
expiry) fill in the axes. O-14 (Michael Pursifull) names the deeper cause: the
board is an authored backdrop (F25), and backdrops do not refresh themselves;
"a stale message is wrong once and a stale board is wrong every time it is
read."

### Recommendation

Never accept a bare "green." Bind a worker's status to a receiver-produced,
observable, provenance-stamped signal that pairs liveness with progress: a
heartbeat carrying a monotonically advancing work counter, plus the observable
artifacts the work should be leaving (tests passing, commits landed, a queue
draining), not a self-rated mood field. This is the watermelon antidote and the
direct answer to R-107, R-16, R-14, and R-93: health is receiver-produced and
stamped with when and by whom it was checked, so the control plane can tell
NOT-CHECKED from CHECKED-AND-FINE. Report status one level up on a fixed beat
and against the declared plan, with the beat tightening for low-readiness
workers (the readiness axis). Make the first status event a confirm-back, so a
misread task is caught before compute is spent. And treat every backdrop the
supervisor leads from (its board) as an artifact that decays silently unless an
instrument re-verifies the world rather than the roster (O-14, the `dsx`
pattern); a status surface with no re-verification path is a watermelon by
construction.

### Carried flag

The Army distinction between a spot report (event-triggered, sent immediately)
and a periodic report (scheduled, recurring) is standardized in FM 6-99, which
was NOT retrieved in this study. The *concept* of event-triggered reporting is
supported by ADP 6-0 para 1-59; the report-format taxonomy must be cited to
FM 6-99 before any downstream use, and never attributed to ADP 6-0 or ADP 6-22.
Separately, the one-on-one engagement multipliers often quoted with the cadence
finding are UNCONFIRMED (secondary aggregators only); the cadence itself
(weekly to biweekly) is supported, the multipliers are not.

## Question 3: Escalation discipline. Decide versus elevate

### Doctrine and workplace levers

Disciplined initiative is the boundary condition: subordinates have a duty to
act within the constraints of the commander's intent, and under mission command
they are "required, not just permitted" to do so when current orders no longer
apply or a threat or opportunity appears [@adp6-0-2019, para 1-59 and 1-61].
Commander's intent is literally the fence: it "defines the limits within which
subordinates may exercise initiative" [@adp6-0-2019, para 1-62 and 1-45].
Doctrine gives two explicit tests plus urgency: act unilaterally only when the
action serves the higher intent and does not desynchronize the operation, and
when the clock forbids coordination; if time permits, coordinate
[@adp6-0-2019, para 1-63 and 1-64]. Reporting a fact is not requesting a
decision: the subordinate decides, acts, then reports [@adp6-0-2019, para 1-59].

The workplace lane makes the fence quantitative and operational. Manage by
exception: define tolerances, act freely inside them, escalate only on a
forecast breach; the point is to keep the level above out of routine decisions
[@prince2_exception]. Incident practice ties a graded severity (by business
impact, written down in advance) to a fixed escalation path and communication
cadence, and auto-advances to the next responder on an acknowledgement timeout
[@pagerduty_severity]. And RACI's consulted-versus-informed line is exactly the
escalate-versus-notify line: consult is block-and-ask (a true escalation),
inform is act-and-tell (a notification) [@wikipedia_ram]. Most escalations that
annoy an operator are informs mislabeled as consults.

### The internal gap this answers

Two recorded shapes. R-109 (Michael Pursifull; finding-006-global-tier /
director#63): the global tier is upward-open and downward-closed except through
the director seat, so cross-team and cross-cluster relay routes through the
director by topology, and a denied cross-team publish returned "context
deadline exceeded" three times, an authorization denial wearing a timeout's
clothes. (R-109 was amended 2026-09-24 on director#77: relay through the
director is now a policy choice expressed in credentials, not a consequence of
topology, and amended R-95 has every seat hold its own credential with a named
refusal at the sender. The recorded instance stands; the topology clause does
not.) A migrated supervisor literally cannot route cross-team without the
director hop, and when the hop is degraded the denial does not announce itself.
R-110 (Michael Pursifull): a director REQUEST carries a reply_by deadline and
confirms delivery or consumption before treating it as in-flight; unbounded
silence to an idle poll-based recipient is undelivered, not pending; the
operator's correction, verbatim, was "wait forever on replies that will never
come." Bound and escalate, do not poll forever. The supporting entries: R-24
(blocked-at-delivery is distinct from failed and from done: work complete, stuck
at the last mile), R-09 (undeliverable must be loud: five messages over three
hours all returned success, none delivered, the human told three times a
session was unresponsive), the composer-wedge danger in O-29 (Michael
Pursifull; idle seats holding stale unsubmitted drafts including a
control-bypass string, so a naive wake would submit them), and the
arcaven-filer wedge (a seat wedged behind a permission dialog only a human can
clear, where escalation to the human is the correct move).

### Recommendation

Give each supervisor an explicit per-decision-class tolerance band (spend,
wall-clock, retries, blast radius) with a consult-or-inform flag, and one rule:
decide inside the band, bound-and-escalate at a forecast breach, never poll
forever. This unifies the doctrine's two tests with the workplace exception
model and answers R-110 directly. Make a denied route or a denied wake fail
loud as a named refusal, never as a timeout or a silent drop, which answers
R-109 and R-09; an authorization denial that reads as a deadline is the worst
available behavior, because it feeds the coordinator false state. Pre-classify
the kinds of trouble a fleet hits by impact and attach each class to a
who-to-wake and a how-often-to-report rule, with an acknowledgement-timeout
auto-escalation for the went-dark case that ends at the human. Recognize
escalate-to-human as a first-class outcome (the arcaven-filer wedge), and
before waking a wedged seat, clear its own pending decision first, so a wake
never submits a stale or control-bypass draft (O-29). Where credential policy
routes a supervisor's cross-team work through the director (R-109 as amended),
treat that path as the escalation path and make its refusals named at the
sender, rather than inferred from a timeout days later.

## Question 4: Delegated decision rights

### Doctrine and workplace levers

Mission command is delegation by design: it empowers subordinate decision
making and decentralized execution appropriate to the situation
[@tc7-22-7-2025, para 2-53]. Mission orders say what and why, not how: they
emphasize "the results to be attained, not how they are to achieve them," carry
"the proper level of detail," and leave the how to the subordinate
[@adp6-0-2019, para 1-53, 1-55, and 1-56]. The load-bearing rule: authority
delegates, responsibility does not. "While commanders can delegate authority,
they cannot delegate responsibility," and some decisions are the commander's
sole responsibility and cannot be delegated at all [@adp6-0-2019, para 1-20 and
1-18]. Levels of control default toward autonomy (the greatest freedom
commensurate with acceptable risk) while keeping an active check, which is the
Army analog of management by exception [@adp6-0-2019, para 1-24 and 1-58]. And
trust sizes the grant: authority is delegated to those whose competence and
judgment have been demonstrated [@adp6-0-2019, para 1-31].

The workplace lane gives the same shape a dial and a table. Delegation is a
continuum of leader behaviors from tell through sell, consult, and join to
delegate within limits, not an on/off switch [@tannenbaum1958leadership].
Decision rights are allocated per decision, with exactly one accountable owner,
via RACI [@wikipedia_ram]. Tolerances are the space inside which a delegate acts
without asking; delegation without a stated tolerance collapses into abdication
or into constant asking [@prince2_exception]. And genuine delegation, matched to
readiness, is how capability is built, not only control hygiene
[@spreitzer1995empowerment; @hersey1969management].

### The internal gap this answers

R-102 (Michael Pursifull, JUDGMENT on observed basis) is the clearest
statement in the corpus of the line: where the director holds direct capability
for a director-level action, do it; reserve relay for work that must run in
another session; a director-level action is scaling a role, writing bd,
authoring a doc, not doing a worker's coding; the bias is toward the role's own
verbs, not toward absorbing the work. O-8 (Michael Pursifull), read as a
decision-rights failure: "proceed" was underspecified against a set with a
policy boundary running through it, and the supervisor resolved it toward the
larger set (including production) with no human in between; it lacked both the
decision right and a commander's-intent statement telling it where the boundary
was, so it filled the gap with its own board. The positive model to preserve is
R-25 (Michael Pursifull; O-6, O-10, O-11): a session did four of five delegated
things, refused the fifth with a reason, verified the refused item read-only,
and reported the residual as zero; a boolean ack would have destroyed all of
that, and partial compliance is first-class. R-21 (Michael Pursifull) adds that
a parked ask's wake condition is not always a human decision, so the model must
not assume the human is the only thing an ask waits on. O-22/R-70 is the
precondition (a delegated decision needs a bounded role to be delegated into),
and O-29 danger is the enactment gap (subordinate decisions stranded in
composers with no reliable path to enact them; a delegated decision with no
enactment path is not delegated, it is stalled).

### Recommendation

Express delegated work as mission orders (result and purpose, not procedure)
plus a decision-rights table: for each decision class the worker faces, name
the accountable owner and whether the worker may decide, must consult, or only
informs. The table is machine-representable and doubles as the escalation policy
from Question 3, because the tolerance band and the delegation grant are two
readings of the same number. Name the small set of non-delegable decisions up
front (the production-protection boundary that O-8 crossed is the exact case),
and encode that the supervisor holds delegated authority while the human or
director retains responsibility. Size each worker's band to its demonstrated
readiness per task class, defaulting to freedom with an active check rather than
per-step approval, and widen the band as trust accrues (this is how capability
is built, not only how it is contained). Bias the supervisor toward its own
role-verbs rather than absorbing a worker's work (R-102). Preserve partial
compliance as a reportable outcome (R-25), never coerce it to a boolean. And
give every delegated decision a reliable enactment path, so a reached decision
is not stranded in a composer (O-29); a decision the fleet cannot safely enact
is a decision not delegated.

## The single strongest recommendation per question

- **Leadership:** make "work completed through others" the supervisor's success
  metric and the thing it is evaluated on, separate its fixed identity from its
  trainable competencies, and route its development through a named channel
  that parallels the task chain. This answers the self-cloning failure
  (O-22/R-70) and the deciding-as-if-leadership-equals-authority failure (O-8).
- **Status:** never accept a bare "green"; bind status to a receiver-produced,
  observable, provenance-stamped liveness-plus-progress signal reported one-up
  on a fixed beat against the plan, with a confirm-back as the first event. This
  answers the register's densest cluster (R-107, R-89, R-16, R-14, R-93, O-14).
- **Escalation:** give each supervisor an explicit per-decision-class tolerance
  band with a consult-or-inform flag; decide inside it, bound-and-escalate at a
  forecast breach, never poll forever, and fail a denied route or wake loud.
  This answers R-110, R-109, R-09, and R-24.
- **Delegation:** express work as mission orders plus a decision-rights table
  sized to demonstrated readiness, name the non-delegable decisions up front,
  keep authority delegated while responsibility stays home, and give every
  delegated decision a reliable enactment path. This answers R-102, O-8, R-25,
  and O-29.
