Three Things, Never Fused

A design party · 2026-09-18

Every session on the host answered to one name, and the director's seat was just an address anyone could occupy. The room pulled three concepts apart and gave the crossing the design it front-ran.

The verdict. Ship the physical-access scheme now, name the crypto trajectory. A session's name is assigned at spawn, not taken from the OS user. The director seat is a fenced lease, acquired and never inherited. Authority is proven by a recency token while every host is the human's own; the signature waits for the second operator. The crossing re-clears with three clean actions, not an address grab.

The crossing enable is those three, kept separate. It front-ran the design because "resume the seat" meant grabbing the one colliding address.

Ships now (physical-access phase)

  • ID-A: launcher-assigned name, strict-mcp-config, per-session durable
  • The seat as a fenced KV lease: create-only single-holder, fencing token
  • Shim-driven renewal (SEAT-D), fail-closed (SEAT-E)
  • Acquire-not-inherit shiftchange, two-phase terminal marker
  • Launcher as minting authority; writer-side deregister

Named, deferred (crypto trajectory)

  • ID-B: self-minted DID + JWS-signed AgentCard
  • Signed principal on the envelope (R-01)
  • Nonrepudiation log (R-05)
  • Receipt-based liveness as doctrine (R-88)
  • Returns when the boundary leaves the host (a second operator)

Sol

Leases and fencing

The seat is a lease, not a name. Create-only KV gives single-holder for free; a dead holder's silence is the release. The one I die on: renewal runs on the shim's timer, never on the model calling a tool, or a long turn vacates a held seat.

Ida

The principal model

Address, seat, durable identity: three things the michael bug wore as one hat. ID-A now, the DID attaches to the same name later. The cluster label is not the session id is not the seat key. The crossing touches all three without fusing them.

Vere

Security and nonrepudiation

The fencing token proves recency, not identity. Write down why that is enough: multi-host is not multi-trust-boundary while every host is the human's. The signature lands with the second operator, and the axis is named so the token is not a dead end.

Hollis

The human's seat

A stuck lease that locks the human out is worse than a brief double-address. The lease frees a dead holder promptly and guarantees one holder; a live-but-useless holder is a human override, not an automatic fence. That honesty is what let me sign it.

Prue

Proportionality and doctrine

There are no peers. The receiver's only question is whether the human's director authorized this, and in this phase the minting authority is the launcher and the grant is the fencing token. The crossing needs three things, not the whole durable plane. Build the crypto plane when the boundary leaves the host, not before.

The two real arguments

Hollis feared a wedged holder. Sol split it: a dead holder ages out on the TTL, a disconnected holder fails closed, and a live-but-useless holder is a human override, not something the lease pretends to solve. SEAT-D keeps a long model turn from looking wedged when it is only thinking.

Vere wanted the signed principal now, at the crossing. Prue drew the line: the crossing is multi-host but single-trust-boundary, because every host is the human's own. The signature waits for a second operator; the fencing token is proportionate until then.

Decided, held for the operator, and fed to the director requirements. Minimal crossing unblock: ID-A, the lease acquisition, writer-side deregister. One operator call left open: the minimal spawn token now, or with the dedicated identity study.
